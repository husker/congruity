**Problem:** Develop a novel lossless compression scheme for multitrack music stems (the separated audio tracks of a song: typically vocals, drums, bass, and other instruments) that beats current state-of-the-art per-stem codecs (FLAC, WavPack, ALAC) in total compression ratio by exploiting inter-stem correlations. The bottleneck: existing lossless audio codecs compress each track independently and cannot leverage the fact that stems within a single song share tempo, key, structural sections (verse / chorus / bridge), and acoustic context, so a kick-drum hit predicts a bass note in time, a chorus drum loop predicts the next chorus drum loop, and harmonic content in one stem predicts harmonic content in another. Find compositions that either (a) bridge multitrack lossless audio compression with concepts from adjacent fields where the connection has not been explicitly developed — e.g. video inter-frame prediction (where neighboring frames predict the next), multispectral / hyperspectral image compression (where bands share spatial structure), multichannel biosignal compression (EEG channels share rhythmic structure), music source separation models (which already model stem dependencies), neural audio codecs (Encodec, SoundStream, DAC, which learn compact latent representations of audio), music structure analysis (which models large-scale repetition and motifs), point-cloud attribute compression (where multiple attributes share geometry), seismic / geophysical multichannel coding, or transform coding with cross-channel decorrelation — or (b) compose two underconnected audio-coding or signal-coding papers that together suggest a new multitrack scheme. Goal: surface candidate research directions concrete enough to implement on MUSDB18-HQ (150 multitrack songs, four stems each, lossless WAV) and benchmark total compressed bytes against per-stem FLAC.
